# Working

*You are in the job. The technical side is going fine. The parts that are hard are the ones nobody trained you for: vague asks, metrics nobody agrees on, analyses that get delivered and then nothing happens.*

## What this phase is about
Closing the gap between doing analysis and being useful. In the matrix, the stages where you were trained (4–6) are not the stages where most of the value is created (1–3, 7–8). The first three years are about becoming good at the second group.

## Read in this order
1. [Stage 1 — Receive the ask](../workflow.md#stage-1--receive-the-ask) and [Stage 3 — Define metrics](../workflow.md#stage-3--define-metrics-and-success). The two stages most often skipped by people who can already write SQL, and the two that most often sink a project.
2. [Stage 8 — Drive the decision](../workflow.md#stage-8--drive-the-decision-and-measure-the-outcome). If your analyses are being delivered but not acted on, this is why.
3. [Collaboration & influence](../competencies/collaboration.md) — the whole file. It is the most pervasive ability in the workflow and the one with the least formal training.
4. [Working with AI](../competencies/working-with-ai.md) — to move from using agents for syntax to using them for the thinking you would otherwise skip.

## Habits that compound
- **Scope in writing**, every time, even for a two-hour ask. Two sentences in a channel.
- **Predict before you query.** Write the expected number down. The surprise is the finding.
- **Keep a decision log**: every analysis, what decision it fed, what happened. This is your evidence at review time and your story bank for the next interview.
- **Follow up on everything you deliver**, two weeks later. "Did this change anything?" is the question that turns an analyst into someone people come back to.
- **Review someone else's work** every week, and have yours reviewed.

## What good looks like
The mid column of each ability table in [`competencies/`](../competencies/). The shift from junior to mid is mostly in Stages 1–3 and 8, not in Stage 5.

## Interview preparation (for the next move)
By now the cases are easy and the behavioural questions are what differentiate. Your [decision log](#habits-that-compound) is the raw material. Make sure your stories cover disagreement, a project that did not land, and influencing a decision you did not own — see the [story bank template](../interview/behavioural-story-bank-template.md).

## Career capital at this phase

This is where trust is built or not. The people who will get you your next two jobs are the manager and the senior peers you work with now, and what they remember is whether your numbers held up, whether you delivered when you said, and whether you told them the bad news yourself. Work in the open: reviewed queries, documented analyses, a decision log with outcomes you can show. Start teaching internally — a short write-up of how you solved something is both documentation and a work sample. See [career capital](../career/career-capital.md).

## The trap
Becoming the person who is very fast at Stages 4–6 and is therefore handed more and more Stage 4–6 work. Fast execution is valued; it is also how you get stuck.
