# Advancing

*You are moving from doing the work to shaping it: deciding what gets analysed, how the team defines success, what the organisation measures. Your output is increasingly other people's decisions.*

## What this phase is about
At senior level the matrix inverts. Technical craft becomes something you review rather than produce; business acumen and influence become most of the job. The hard part is that you got here by being good at the first thing, and the organisation will keep asking you for it.

## Read in this order
1. The **senior column** of every ability table in [`competencies/`](../competencies/). Read them together; the pattern across them is the job description.
2. [Stage 2 — Frame the question](../stages/02-frame-the-question.md), again, but now as the person who *reshapes* the ask rather than refines it — including declining work that will not change a decision.
3. [Stage 3 — Define metrics](../stages/03-define-metrics.md) as the owner of the glossary rather than a contributor to it.
4. [Working with AI](../competencies/working-with-ai.md), the evaluation section: deciding what your team may delegate and what verification it requires is now your call.

## What changes
- **You choose the questions.** Stakeholders come to you before they have formed the ask. The leverage is in saying what should be analysed, not in analysing it.
- **You set standards.** What counts as a verified query, a defensible conclusion, a finished memo. Your team's quality is your output.
- **You own the measurement conversation.** What the organisation tracks, how success is defined, which metrics are retired. This is where business acumen stops being a skill and becomes a responsibility.
- **You decide what the agents do.** Which tasks are delegated, which are not, and how the team evaluates the tools it uses.
- **Your stories change.** Impact is now measured in decisions changed and people developed, not analyses delivered.

## What good looks like
The senior column — and then the question each table does not ask: what would the *next* column say? Writing it for yourself is a useful exercise.

## Interview preparation
Senior interviews test judgment and influence, usually through a case where the correct answer is "I would push back on the question" and a behavioural round focused on conflict, prioritisation, and developing others. The [collaboration](../competencies/collaboration.md) interview lens is the core; the [running example](../running-example/) is useful as something to critique rather than reproduce.

## The trap
Staying the best analyst on the team. If you are still the person who writes the hardest query, you have not advanced; you have been promoted.
