# Reproducing

*You are hiring, onboarding, mentoring, or building a team. The question is no longer whether you can do the job but whether you can transmit it.*

## What this phase is about
Making the six abilities learnable by other people. Most of what senior analysts know is tacit — picked up from projects that went wrong — and most of it never gets written down. This repo exists partly as an attempt to fix that; this page is about using it to do so.

## Using this repo to onboard
- **Week one:** the [README](../README.md) and the [running example](../running-example/). The goal is a shared vocabulary — "that's a Stage 3 problem" should mean something by Friday.
- **First month:** one real ask, taken through all eight stages with a review at each. [Stage 2](../workflow.md#stage-2--frame-the-question) and [Stage 7](../workflow.md#stage-7--recommend-and-communicate) are where the reviews matter most.
- **First quarter:** the junior column of each [competency](../competencies/) table as a shared, explicit expectation — written down, not assumed.

## Using it to review
The four-section structure of each stage (what it is · hard skills · judgment · interview lens) doubles as a review rubric. When an analysis comes to you, ask the stage questions: was the ask framed? was the metric agreed? was the data reconciled? is there a recommendation, and would the analyst defend it? Reviewing against the workflow rather than against the output teaches the workflow.

## Using it to hire
- The [matrix](../README.md#2--abilities-by-stage-the-matrix) is a hiring rubric: decide which abilities the role needs at which level, then design the loop to probe those stages. Most loops over-test Stage 5 and under-test Stages 1, 3, and 8.
- The interview lens in each stage and each competency file is a question bank with "what good looks like" attached, which is the part most question banks lack.
- Take-homes: expect AI use; score the verification. See [Working with AI](../competencies/working-with-ai.md).

## Using it to mentor
- Have people write their own [decision log](working.md#habits-that-compound) and review it with them quarterly. It surfaces which stages they avoid.
- Pair on Stage 1 conversations. Framing is learned by watching someone do it, not by reading about it.
- Ask "what would change the decision?" until they start asking it themselves.

## What good looks like
A team that can describe the job in the same words, review each other's work against the same stages, and tell you honestly which stages it is weak at. And a successor.

## Career capital at this phase

By now the strongest evidence of your ability is other people: the analysts you developed who went on to do well, the team whose standards you set. Public work belongs here — writing, speaking, a shared body of knowledge, a repo like this one — because you have enough to say and enough standing to be heard. It is also how the chain of trust extends past the people you have personally worked with. See [career capital](../career/career-capital.md).

## Contributing back
If you adapt this material for a team, the adaptations are the most valuable thing you can contribute: which questions worked, which stages needed more, what you had to add. See [Contributing](../README.md#contributing).
